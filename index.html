What did the calculator assume?

Defaults: retire at 65, fund spending to 90, 2% yearly inflation and 4% annual net investment return. The spending baseline is the 2023 survey’s $1,384 per member, adjusted by 2% a year to 2026. Your optional spending amount is treated as today’s prices. These are model assumptions, not promised returns.

Without balances, CPF starts at $0. All future OA contributions are assumed used for housing, so the salary-only CPF estimate mainly counts SA contributions. Enter your actual OA, SA and housing payment to refine it. Salary stays constant; the $8,000 monthly wage ceiling and 2026 age-based allocations are held constant. OA grows at 2.5%, SA at 4%. Extra interest, bonuses, MA overflow and contributions after 55 are excluded.

The FRS scenario grows 2026’s $220,400 by an assumed 3.5% a year until you turn 55. ERS comparison is twice that scenario. Future sums are not announced this far ahead. ERS requires an eligible voluntary commitment after 55.

The income reference caps age-55 retirement savings at the modelled FRS, scales them back to 2026 FRS terms, interpolates CPF Board’s male Standard Plan payout examples starting at 65, then scales the income back by the same retirement-sum factor. This is a scenario comparison, not CPF Board’s personalised projection: sex, future pricing and rule changes can change the result. It assumes OA is available at 55. Amounts above FRS are not automatically counted as spendable investments or committed to ERS.

Spending rises after retirement while the reference CPF payout stays flat. Each monthly investment shortfall is discounted at 4%, including any bridge before 65. Existing cash investments grow at 4%; the result is the additional fixed monthly investment needed. CPF target-gap amounts are separate illustrations at 4% and should not be added blindly to the investment amount: a CPF top-up changes the income scenario.

Put the pieces together

Your funding order, from payday to retirement

  1. 1. Keep your base safe.

    Clear costly debt and keep emergency and near-term housing cash accessible. Decide what you can commit for decades.

  2. 2. Check compulsory CPF before adding more.

    The starting estimate above shows what salary contributions may already achieve. FRS is a useful income-floor checkpoint; ERS is an optional higher-income commitment at 55+, not a requirement for everyone. If you already project to meet your target, extra top-ups need a clear purpose.

  3. 3. Close a chosen CPF gap early, when affordable.

    Use the early-top-up example in your results. More years of interest helps, but cash top-ups and OA-to-SA/RA transfers are generally irreversible. Check the actual top-up limit in CPF; a top-up ceiling and a tax-relief ceiling are different things.

  4. 4. Decide whether SRS earns its place.

    For citizens/PRs, the cap is $15,300 per year—equivalent to saving $1,275 a month. It is not $15,300 a month. SRS is optional: its value depends on your marginal tax rate, other reliefs, liquidity and withdrawal tax. Total personal income tax relief is capped at $80,000 per year.

    Where to invest SRS money

    Contributing alone leaves cash uninvested. For a long horizon, consider a diversified, low-cost equity fund eligible for SRS, such as an SRS-supported fund portfolio through Endowus; compare fund and platform fees. SRS cannot simply buy any overseas ETF, so do not assume VWRA is available. For near-term spending, assess eligible deposits, SSBs or suitable bonds instead.

    How SRS withdrawals work

    Normal qualifying retirement withdrawals are 50% taxable and can generally be spread across 10 years. Early withdrawals are generally fully taxable plus a 5% penalty, subject to exceptions. Your qualifying retirement age depends on the age prevailing at your first SRS contribution.

    Example SRS investment routes ↗ · IRAS SRS contribution rules ↗
  5. 5. Invest outside CPF / SRS for flexibility.

    Use the monthly investment illustration above as a starting point, then verify your income with CPF before committing. If you use SRS investments for part of that gap, count those investments once, and allow for withdrawal tax and access dates. Keep an accessible bridge if retiring before CPF LIFE payouts begin.

  6. 6. Gradually prepare cash and bond maturities.

    As spending dates approach, build your chosen cash buffer and fixed-income ladder. Keep long-horizon growth money invested at a risk level you can tolerate, then review the plan once a year.

Make investments easier to spend later

A drawdown plan for the years after work.

If you hold a broad share fund such as VWRA, build a written transition plan over time. A retirement date does not require selling everything at once.

01

Calculate the gap first

Use the monthly plan above. Size investments against spending left after CPF LIFE and any other steady income.

02

Set an accessible spending reserve

Choose how many months of your investment-funded gap to hold in cash or similarly accessible savings, based on comfort and upcoming costs.

03

Build a fixed-income ladder gradually

Match maturities to planned spending. Singapore Savings Bonds can be redeemed monthly without a penalty; allow for processing time, so they are not instant cash.

04

Keep long-horizon money invested

Keep an equity allocation you can tolerate through market falls. Rebalance on a set schedule and use a written rule for replenishing cash after stronger markets.
