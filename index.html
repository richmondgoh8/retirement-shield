What did the calculator assume?

Defaults: retire at 65, fund spending to 90 and use 4% annual net investment return. The helper example starts at $3,500 in 2026 prices with 3% general inflation. It keeps the normal $300 helper levy fixed and adds an age-related care allowance after 65, calibrated to the category midpoints ($3,090 at 65 and $4,260 ten years later). The same age trend is extrapolated after 75; actual care needs can differ substantially. Your optional spending amount is treated as today’s prices. These are model assumptions, not promised returns.

Without balances, CPF starts at $0. All future OA contributions are assumed used for housing, so the salary-only CPF estimate mainly counts SA contributions. Enter your actual OA, SA and housing payment to refine it. Salary stays constant; the $8,000 monthly wage ceiling and 2026 age-based allocations are held constant. OA grows at 2.5%, SA at 4%. Extra interest, bonuses, MA overflow and contributions after 55 are excluded.

The FRS scenario grows 2026’s $220,400 by an assumed 3.5% a year until you turn 55. ERS comparison is twice that scenario. Future sums are not announced this far ahead. ERS requires an eligible voluntary commitment after 55.

The salary path uses projected age-55 savings up to FRS. The FRS and ERS comparisons assume their respective target is fully funded. Each balance is scaled back to 2026 FRS terms, interpolated against CPF Board’s male Standard Plan examples (up to ERS), then its payout is scaled back by the same retirement-sum factor. Payouts start at 65 and stay level in this illustration. This is a scenario comparison, not CPF Board’s personalised projection: sex, future pricing and rule changes can change the result. It assumes OA is available at 55. Projected CPF above the target is not automatically counted as spendable investments. The FRS and ERS comparisons explicitly require funding any target shortfall; the app does not assume that money appears automatically.

Spending follows the chosen inflation and care example after retirement while the reference CPF payout stays flat. The helper model retains the normal levy throughout: it does not assume future eligibility for the concession or automatically deduct MediSave or subsidies. Each monthly investment shortfall is discounted at 4%, including any bridge before 65. Existing cash investments grow at 4%; the result is the additional fixed monthly investment needed. The selected path shows two separate pots: extra money earmarked to reach its CPF target by 55, and an outside-CPF portfolio for the remaining spending. The first pot assumes 4% while accumulating; eligibility may require holding it outside CPF until a permitted transfer or top-up. Contributions stop by the earlier of retirement or 55, then compound to 55. Existing accessible assets are allocated only to the outside portfolio. The comparison does not automatically redirect the CPF funding amount into investments after 55.

Count food once

Groceries cover both people. The helper line excludes food. At 65 it allows S$650–850 salary + S$300 levy + S$200–300 for insurance, check-ups and other non-food employment provisions.

Check the levy at 67

Current MOM rules offer an eligible Singapore citizen employer aged 67+ a S$60 levy, rather than S$300. That is S$240 less a month. On that basis, the age-75 range is S$3,390–4,650. The main model retains S$300; future rules and eligibility can change.

What is included in the helper provision?

Budget for salary, levy, required insurance, six-monthly medical examinations, medical costs and a reserve for home leave or employment costs. Food is already in groceries. Actual agency charges and replacement or respite support can require separate cash. A refundable security bond is not the same as a monthly expense.

At 65, an independent employer does not qualify for the elderly levy concession by age alone; the current age threshold is 67. Disability-based eligibility is separate. Future salary and insurance allowances are planning estimates.

Optional tax wrapperWhere SRS fits into your investment potEligibility, investment choices and withdrawals

SRS is part of the outside-CPF portfolio in this plan. Count each investment once, and allow for withdrawal tax and access dates. The illustration does not assume a tax saving or automatic SRS contribution.

For citizens/PRs, the cap is $15,300 per year—equivalent to saving $1,275 a month. It is not $15,300 a month. SRS is optional: its value depends on your marginal tax rate, other reliefs, liquidity and withdrawal tax. Total personal income tax relief is capped at $80,000 per year.

Where to invest SRS money

Contributing alone leaves cash uninvested. For a long horizon, consider a diversified, low-cost equity fund eligible for SRS, such as an SRS-supported fund portfolio through Endowus; compare fund and platform fees. SRS cannot simply buy any overseas ETF, so do not assume VWRA is available. For near-term spending, assess eligible deposits, SSBs or suitable bonds instead.

How SRS withdrawals work

Normal qualifying retirement withdrawals are 50% taxable and can generally be spread across 10 years. Early withdrawals are generally fully taxable plus a 5% penalty, subject to exceptions. Your qualifying retirement age depends on the age prevailing at your first SRS contribution.

Set an accessible spending reserve

Choose how many months of your investment-funded gap to hold in cash or similarly accessible savings, based on comfort and upcoming costs.

Build a fixed-income ladder gradually

Match maturities to planned spending. Singapore Savings Bonds can be redeemed monthly without a penalty; allow for processing time, so they are not instant cash.

04

Keep long-horizon money invested

Keep an equity allocation you can tolerate through market falls. Rebalance on a set schedule and use a written rule for replenishing cash after stronger markets.
