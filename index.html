Learn the wrapper

Different wrappers. Different trade-offs.

The brand name is not the comparison. First identify what is inside: a participating fund, a market-linked fund, or money that stays more accessible.

Participating / retirement insurance01

AIA Retirement Saver (IV)

A retirement insurance plan that can provide a scheduled income stream. The important split is between guaranteed benefits and non-guaranteed dividends or bonuses.

In plain English

“You are trading some flexibility for a pre-set income shape.”

Investment-linked policy02

Any ILP, including retirement-branded plans

An ILP combines insurance with investments in sub-funds. The value can move with markets and charges may be taken from units over time.

In plain English

“You are buying a policy plus an investment account. You carry the investment risk.”

Market-linked retirement plan03

Manulife SmartRetire (V)

A whole-life, regular-premium ILP designed around a target retirement age, with choices for an income stream or lump sum. Version and terms matter.

In plain English

“Bonuses and flexibility do not remove the need to check fees, risk and the exit path.”

Product names are examples, not recommendations. Always check the version, Product Summary, policy illustration, Product Highlights Sheet and policy contract for your own quote.

A KEY CPF MILESTONE

What changes at 55?

A Retirement Account is created. Savings move from SA first, then OA, up to the applicable Full Retirement Sum. Your own cohort’s FRS is set when you turn 55; the 2026 figure above is for that cohort only.

OA + SA→Retirement Account→CPF LIFE payouts

Pause before topping up: CPF cash top-ups and transfers to RA are generally irreversible and reserved for retirement payouts. Check housing plans, emergency cash and eligibility first.

A simple retirement structure

Build two buckets around the job each dollar has.

There is no universal gold standard allocation. A useful starting point is to separate reliable spending money from flexible money, then size both around your spending, health, housing and family needs.

01 · PAY THE FLOOR

Reliable income and near-term cash

Cover essential monthly bills first with predictable income. Keep money for the next few years of planned costs accessible and low volatility.

  • CPF LIFE for lifelong income, based on your chosen plan.
  • Cash or short dated deposits / government securities for emergencies and known expenses.
  • Review payout start age, liquidity, rates, eligibility and guarantee terms.

Ask: “How many months of essential spending can I meet without selling investments?”

02 · FUND THE REST

Flexible, longer-term money

Keep a diversified growth pool for inflation protection, later life goals and discretionary spending. Expect values and income to move around.

  • Use a mix you can hold through market falls; avoid relying on one fund or dividend stream.
  • Choose a scheduled withdrawal amount and review it yearly against portfolio value.
  • Understand fees, tax, currency exposure and how withdrawals affect capital.

Ask: “If markets fell sharply, could I reduce withdrawals or use the other bucket?”

This is a planning framework, not an allocation prescription. The right cash reserve and investment mix depend on your situation and willingness to adjust spending.

Income tools, with the fine print visible

A payout button does not make income guaranteed.

Endowus and Syfe can route investment distributions to a bank account. The amount can change, the fund value can fall, and a payout may include money that could otherwise stay invested.

Lifetime income

CPF LIFE

Government-backed lifelong payouts based on your CPF savings, start age and chosen plan.

  • Payouts continue for life.
  • Escalating plan starts lower and grows 2% yearly.
  • Best used as a baseline for essential recurring expenses.

Investment distributions

Endowus Income

Income portfolios use investment funds and show a current payout target. Distributions depend on fund holdings and are not guaranteed.

  • Check the live target, fees and portfolio risk.
  • Cash, CPF and SRS payout handling can differ by portfolio.
  • A distribution is not the same thing as total investment return.

Make future-you’s life easier

Set up an exit system before it gets hard to track.

The safest retirement plan is one you, or someone you trust, can understand later. Keep a short inventory and a repeatable review date.

ONE PAGE+ONE TRUSTED PERSON+ONE REVIEW DATE

Annual “still right for me?” check

  1. 1

    List every policy, CPF LIFE plan, investment account and bank account.

  2. 2

    Write down the provider, purpose, current value, payout, fees and withdrawal steps.

  3. 3

    Store statements and contact details securely. Do not put passwords or OTPs in the list.

  4. 4

    Choose a trusted person who knows where the list is and how to contact providers.

  5. 5

    Review beneficiaries, CPF nomination, bank details and recurring transfers after major life changes.

Never share Singpass credentials, passwords or one-time codes. Use official provider contact channels.
