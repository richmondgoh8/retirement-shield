What did the calculator assume?

All projected money amounts are displayed in today’s purchasing power, using your selected inflation to remove general price increases. Current salary, balances and monthly savings are amounts today. Defaults: retire at 65, fund spending to 90 and use 4% nominal annual net investment return. The helper example starts at $3,500 in 2026 prices with 3% general inflation. It keeps the normal $300 helper levy fixed and adds an age-related care allowance after 65, calibrated to the category midpoints ($3,090 at 65 and about $3,170 at 75, both in today’s purchasing power). The same age trend is extrapolated after 75; actual care needs can differ substantially. Your optional spending amount is treated as today’s prices. The S$3,500 starting budget includes a cushion above the S$3,090 category midpoint; neither is an official cost standard. These are model assumptions, not promised returns. Educational illustration, not personalised financial advice.

Without balances, CPF starts at $0. By default, future OA contributions are assumed used for housing, so the salary-only CPF estimate mainly counts SA contributions. Enter your actual OA, SA and housing payment to refine it (or click "Set housing deduction to $0" if your flat is paid up). Under 2025/2026 CPF rules, your Special Account (SA) closes at age 55; balances up to FRS form your Retirement Account (RA) earning at least 4% p.a., with any excess moving to OA (2.5%) unless topped up to the Enhanced Retirement Sum (ERS). Extra interest, bonuses, and MA overflow are excluded for conservative planning.

The FRS scenario grows the published 2026 sum of $220,400 by an assumed 3.5% a year until 55, then converts that future target to today’s purchasing power for display. ERS is twice that scenario (published 2026 amount: $440,800). Future sums are not announced this far ahead. ERS requires an eligible voluntary commitment after 55.

The salary path uses projected age-55 savings up to FRS. The FRS and ERS comparisons assume their respective target is fully funded. The reference table is for a male member aged 55 in 2026; individual payouts differ. Scaling to future cohorts and interpolating between balances are approximations. Payouts start at 65. The Standard Plan holds payouts flat, while the Escalating Plan option starts ~20% lower and compounds at 2% yearly. This is a scenario comparison, not CPF Board’s personalised projection.

Optional tax wrapperWhere SRS fits into your investment potEligibility, investment choices and withdrawals

SRS is part of the outside-CPF portfolio in this plan. Count each investment once, and allow for withdrawal tax and access dates. The illustration does not assume a tax saving or automatic SRS contribution.

For citizens/PRs, the cap is $15,300 per year—equivalent to saving $1,275 a month. Total personal income tax relief is capped at $80,000 per year.

Where to invest SRS money

Contributing alone leaves cash uninvested. For a long horizon, consider a diversified, low-cost equity fund eligible for SRS, through an eligible provider; compare fund and platform fees. SRS cannot simply buy any overseas ETF, so check each investment’s SRS eligibility. For near-term spending, assess eligible deposits, SSBs or suitable bonds instead.

How SRS withdrawals work

Normal qualifying retirement withdrawals are 50% taxable and can generally be spread across 10 years. Early withdrawals are generally fully taxable plus a 5% penalty, subject to exceptions. Your qualifying retirement age depends on the age prevailing at your first SRS contribution.

Set an accessible spending reserve

Choose how many months of your investment-funded gap to hold in cash or similarly accessible savings, based on comfort and upcoming costs.

03

Build a fixed-income ladder gradually

Match maturities to planned spending. Singapore Savings Bonds can be redeemed monthly without a penalty; allow for processing time, so they are not instant cash.

04

Keep long-horizon money invested

Keep an equity allocation you can tolerate through market falls. Rebalance on a set schedule and use a written rule for replenishing cash after stronger markets.
